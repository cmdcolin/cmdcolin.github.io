---
title: All the screenshots in your documentation should be autogenerated
date: 2026-07-31
---

People that make complex applications may, over time, make custom little
screenshots for bespoke tutorials or emails to their boss to say "look at this
cool feature we have!"

This is nice, but if you are like me and are a small team, you may also have a
small library of examples in your head where you know your app can do X, Y, and
Z and you never had time to make a tutorial for it. To externalize this
brain-only content, you need to make your entire documentation system more
automated.

Specifically: you need to make screenshots of high value items in your
application in a 100% automated way. Not just for snapshot testing, but for your
docs.[^rr]

## Make a helper app

I recommend using AI agents to help you make a bunch of puppeteer scripts to do

I also recommend making your AI agent whip up a small 'review' webapp where you
can review updated screenshots. Make it show you when a figure gets updated.
Make it have a textfield so you can specify if there is something wrong with the
screenshot. Then the website updates a review.json file, and you feed that back
to the AI.

The screenshots can also get an extra layer of 'markup' added to them (text
blurbs, etc) which can be tricky, but here again, accessibility helps to locate
elements on the page

![Review app showing each updated screenshot with a textfield for feedback, saved to review.json](/screenshot-reviews.png)

## There are many benefits to this type of workflow

- It forces you to exercise real high-value end-to-end user workflows that you
  want people to use. Similar to the
  [Beyoncé rule](https://abseil.io/resources/swe-book/html/ch11.html) from
  Google
- It forces you to work on accessibility, because you should make your puppeteer
  scripts use simple selectors, not complicated ones
- It forces you to think about navigating complex states within your app, and
  even better, forces you to confront issues that users face: are there easier
  ways to get to this state in my app?

## It really makes ya think

<https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QhnsC2V4_0w>

- Can I declaratively specify the entire state of my app with ease? This is
  something that can be done with react state managers. There are complex state
  trees that can be serialized to json (mobx-state-tree) or just
  url-is-the-state people like nuqs. But in any case, this can be very handy,
  and avoids lots of puppeteer point-and-click. Your app's only interface might
  not just be point-and-click from a user...agents might use it or other
  programmatic automation may occur

- It makes your GUI documentation amenable to agentic improvement. I can just
  tell Claude "make a new tutorial for X" and voila it's done. No clicking and
  pointing. I additionally tell it to "let the screenshots do the talking,
  absolute minimal prose"

## Conclusion

There is no real excuse not to do this now if you use AI, and the benefits are
likely a big net positive ...now go out there and fix those docs

[^rr]:
    This is the same idea the reproducible research people have had for decades:
    the figure in your paper should be generated by the code in your paper, not
    pasted in from a run nobody can re-do.
