Recently the Software Should Work conference (https://softwareshould.work/) took
place in Columbia MO and they subsequently posted a number of great talks to
their youtube.

There was one talk called 'System Daydreams and Logarithmic Colored Glasses'
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Q4y0xNFoW00&t=468s and suggests that engineers
aren't very good at seeing exponentials emerging in theirwork. It's a fun talk
but I am going to flip the script and point out another fatal bias

Engineers should really careful not to settle for what I'll call "local minima".

And by this I mean, for example, the AI outputs some stuff, it kind of works.
And you are sort of ok with this solution but you keep prompting it and
prompting it to try to shape the results. This is a dangerous pattern, because
once this that first solution is outputted, it has found a local minima, and you
may have a hard time getting out of it (that is, if you can even tell you are in
one)

Every subsequent prompt subsequently affect every single decision that is
downstream from that. It's sort of like laying down the architecture of your
project, but I don't really like the term architecture.

Here are a couple tales about trying to get the best results from your vibe
coding. Maybe it is just storytelling or not a very generalizable lesson but I
wanted to share

## My WebGPU video synthesizer

In the past, I used to make analog video glitch art by alligator clipping
composite video cables together, and seeing what would pop up on a CRT screen.
It was often absolutely insane. I loved it. Unfortunately, after having to move
like 5 times over the years I just dont have the equipment anymore.

It could be seen as a massive and disappointing cheapening of analog video but I
asked Claude one day: Can you use WebGPU to make a realistic analog video mixing
system? I want to emulate effects like dirty video mixing.

I used Claude Fable and it absolutely nailed the project from the very first
prompt in a blank directory. Everything subsequent to that was fine tuning and
fun adjustments to what it laid down.

Fable used WebGPU compute shaders to realistically compute the composite video
waveform signal, and this creates extremely realistic analog glitch effects. The
visual effects are effectively the same that you get from an analog system.

I have seen other peoples projects making (clearly vibecoded) video synths that
try to simulate some analog effects and you can see that they are "glitch
overlays" that paint rainbows and vhs style glitches on top of the footage

The system that I created, or that Claude created, is the real deal.

It is worth saying that before this, I had tried to get Opus to do the a similar
project. At that time it tried using
https://github.com/zhuker/ntsc/blob/master/ntsc.py and I settled for just
creating still images then every subsequent prompt I made just resulted in it
painting "analog effects on top of the photo". This completely failed to make
the analog style glitches that I wanted.

It was only by establishing the proper basis of simulating the composite video
waveform that we were able to proceed with the proper solution. Potentially if I
had prompted Opus a little better I could have gotten a better result, but it is
just a tale that laying down the proper solution from the start is vital.

## Trying to write a paper using Claude

This is an example of a very bad experience. For better or worse, I started
trying to write an entire paper using Claude. I wanted to just bounce some ideas
off of it, and try to get the 'shape' of the paper. I didn't even intend to turn
it in as is. But what followed was a real lesson in local minima I think. It
very quickly was able to spit out an entire essay, that relatively completely
satisfied the shape of the paper I wanted writing, but the tone was so bad, and
so robotic, that no amount of prompting could fix it.

I tried to fix it by hand writing a couple pages, and I would these hand-written
parts to claude, then say "this is a sample of my writing, try to use this style
of writing", but it would not work to patch up the text. Everything was
extremely staccato and 'telegraphic' short sentences that made no sense when
taken in isolation. Claude seemed to have also gotten very fixated on a specific
word count that I suggested early on, but was probably stored in a Claude
'memory' file rather than explicitly written anywhere. In any case, it was just
generating pages upon pages of stuff where the paragraphs would be impossible to
understand without reading the previous one (essays are best often when you can
just skim them and jump to a particular section or paragraph without the entire
previous).

Maybe I wasn't using the right prompts to spruce it up.

But the fact of the matter is that I felt I had fallen into a very bad local
minima. And there was no getting out of it.

So I proceeded to hand-write the paper, like I should have done in the first
place, but I couldn't even refer to the AI generated paper as a guide, because
it was so unreadable it poisoned my own brain against it

## Conclusion

Just like we should aware of these logarithmic scaling factors around us...be
aware of whether you are prompting yourself into a dead end...or a local minima,
instead of the best solution possible. You may need to delete everything and
start over if this occurs
