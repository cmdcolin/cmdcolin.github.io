All the screenshots in your documentation should be autogenerated

2026-07-31

People that make complex applications may, over time, make custom little screenshots for bespoke tutorials or emails to their boss to say “look at this cool feature we have!”

This is nice, but this doesn’t really scale. These single screenshots looked ok in power point slides or to emails to your boss but doesn’t help a wider user community. To externalize this brain-only content, you should to make documentation system more automated.

Specifically: you should make screenshots in your documentation 100% automated. [^rr]

Make a helper app

I recommend using AI agents to help you make a bunch of puppeteer scripts to do this.

I also recommend making your AI agent whip up a small ‘review’ webapp where you can review updated screenshots. Make it show you when a figure gets updated. Make it have a textfield so you can specify if there is something wrong with the screenshot. Then the website updates a review.json file, and you feed that back to the AI.

The screenshots can also get an extra layer of ‘markup’ added to them (text blurbs, etc) which can be tricky, but here again, accessibility helps to locate elements on the page

There are many benefits to this type of workflow

  • It forces you to exercise real high-value end-to-end user workflows that you want people to use. Similar to the Beyoncé rule from Google
  • It forces you to work on accessibility, because you should make your puppeteer scripts use simple selectors, not complicated ones
  • It forces you to think about navigating complex states within your app, and even better, forces you to confront issues that users face: are there easier ways to get to this state in my app?

It really makes ya think

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QhnsC2V4_0w

  • Can I declaratively specify the entire state of my app with ease? This is something that can be done with react state managers. There are complex state trees that can be serialized to json (mobx-state-tree) or just url-is-the-state people like nuqs. But in any case, this can be very handy, and avoids lots of puppeteer point-and-click. Your app’s only interface might not just be point-and-click from a user…agents might use it or other programmatic automation may occur

  • It makes your GUI documentation amenable to agentic improvement. I can just tell Claude “make a new tutorial for X” and voila it’s done. No clicking and pointing. I additionally tell it to “let the screenshots do the talking, absolute minimal prose”

Conclusion

There is no real excuse not to do this now if you use AI, and the benefits are likely a big net positive …now go out there and fix those docs
